## 1. Value proposition (one page)
Our platform helps property professionals publish understandable neighborhood property listings while giving local sponsors a clearly identified, permission-based place to share approved business information. Visitors can browse available homes and see relevant local business information without confusing advertisements with property details.

**Problem (hypotheses, not established stakeholder facts):**
- Property professionals may need one place to create and review property listings.
- Local sponsors may need a simple way to draft accurate promotional content and request approval.
- Visitors may need clear labels and accessible page layouts.

**Who benefits:** Property professionals, local sponsors, and visitors browsing properties.

**Proposed Week 8 value:** A small accessible prototype with sample listings, one sponsor area clearly labeled "Sponsored," and a manual approval status for submitted content. No paid placements, live client integration, or collection of sensitive visitor information.

**Success measures to validate:** (1) Users can locate a sample listing; (2) a reviewer can identify whether content is approved; (3) an unapproved sponsor claim is not publicly displayed; (4) keyboard users can navigate core pages.

## 4. Consent script

"Thank you for speaking with us. This is a voluntary interview for a student project, not a sales call. We will take minimal notes about general workflows and needs. Please do not share private client, financial, or contact information. You may skip questions or stop at any time. Is it okay to continue under those conditions? We will not make an audio or video recording."

**Record:** participant consent only after an approved real interview. No consent has been collected for this draft.

## 5. Eight neutral discovery questions

1. What steps do you currently follow when preparing a listing or business promotion?
2. Which part of that process usually takes the most time?
3. What information do visitors most often need to understand your offering?
4. How do you review content for accuracy before it is published?
5. Who should be allowed to create, edit, and approve content?
6. How would you know whether a new platform was useful to you?
7. What device or accessibility needs should we consider?
8. What is one problem you would prioritize solving first, and why?

**Question review:** Wording avoids assumptions such as "Don't you hate your current tools?" and does not ask for protected, client-identifying, or private data. Independent Gemini critique remains **not performed** and needs to be documented if used.

## 7. Working personas (hypotheses; not demographic stereotypes)

- **Property professional:** Manages draft listing details and wants a clear review step before publication. Needs straightforward editing and a visible publication status. **Evidence:** simulated notes only.
- **Local sponsor:** Creates a short business description and wants to correct outdated information, subject to a reviewer approving claims. **Evidence:** simulated notes only.

## 8. Acceptance and ethical guardrails
- No contact without instructor and participant approval.
- No scraped contacts, automated calls, bulk outreach, or AI impersonation.
- Do not store private customer information, recordings, addresses of private individuals, or credentials in GitHub.
- Treat all simulated findings as hypotheses until validated.
- Label paid or sponsored content plainly and separate it from editorial property details.
